### Added

- `karyos-catalog verify` and the Archive's integrity verifier (SPEC.md §8.4, "Verification"; karyos#665): `karyos_archive::integrity::verify` recomputes each sealed range's digest, walks the chain, checks the key registry and signed heads under a trust root supplied outside the export, compares the chain with its anchors, and reports every range as `verified`, `beyond_anchor`, `unsealed`, `purged` or `inconsistent` with explicit reasons, the first inconsistent record and the signature and anchor coverage with the custody of the signed coverage (a test signer's heads are reported as no custody separation); evidence faults that hold for the whole chain, an anchor that fails validation among them, are reported, fail the command and raise a scrub event even when no sealed range exposes them. Missing trust is `inconsistent` with reason `untrusted_evidence`, apart from proved corruption; an index shortened below a seal is `inconsistent`; only `verified` carries a tamper-evidence statement; `purged` states that it proves nothing about payload bytes; every other outcome is C-EXTRACT's "unverifiable"; time evidence is reported in a separate field. A journal record that does not chain or decode is reported, and nothing from it on is verified, even when no range reaches it; a registry entry the journal commits but the export lacks makes the registry evidence malformed; a revoked key's head counts only with store evidence that it was anchored before the revocation; the registry is replayed with the same sequence, commitment and timing checks as on reopen over the accepted chain prefix only; an anchor counts only from an operator export store whose location the operator states (`--anchors HEX=DIR`), and a malformed anchor entry is malformed evidence; a seal larger than its recording is `inconsistent` without being read; a directory that does not exist, a store that cannot be opened or read, and a report that cannot be written are errors. A configurable scrub reverifies on a schedule and raises an event per mismatch and per journal fault.
