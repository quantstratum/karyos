### Added

- The service's atomic snapshot callback (R-CORE-9, karyos#746): `ClusteredService::snapshot` writes the service's state as one or more versioned parts into a `ServiceSnapshot` (bytes and part slots allocated once; `ServiceSnapshot::reserved_bytes` is the budget with 48 bytes of metadata per part), and `ClusteredService::restore` takes them back, all or nothing; both default to a service with no state. `ServiceContainer::snapshot` composes, between two applies, the sessions, timers and guards sections with the service's parts, all from the last applied index (`ComposedSnapshot::index`), and `ServiceContainer::restore` checks every container section, hands the service its parts, and only then replaces its tables, so a refused restore changes nothing (`RestoreError`, `RestoreRefused` for an unknown part, a newer version, a missing or malformed part). Every container table comes from the snapshot's one position: a snapshot without a guards section restores a fresh guard table, the session bootstrap flags (bounds applied, no registration yet) travel in the timers section, logged session bounds restore over the container's configured ones within its session budget (decoded within it, before allocating), and unlogged ones must match. A section of a kind the store does not know is now `SnapshotError::UnknownSection`, reported only once the whole snapshot's digest holds (a failed digest stays damage), and a reopened store lists such snapshots in the new `Opened::unknown`, apart from `damaged`. A sim test over C2's crashing disk shows a composed snapshot of three service parts leaves, at every crash point and failed flush, the previous snapshot or the complete new one, restoring to the same state as replaying the log. CRC-32C checks detect corruption and claim no tamper evidence.
